The epidemiology of influenza has shifted in recent years due to COVID-19 social restrictions. However, it remains unclear whether these findings apply to China, where a strict Zero-COVID policy was implemented. We evaluated influenza trends in Zhejiang Province, China from 2010–2023 – before, during, and after the strict policy. Specifically, using weekly surveillance data (2010–2023), we applied change-point detection to identify structural shifts and interrupted time-series models to quantify policy impacts by comparing predicted (P) and observed (O) influenza cases based on differences (O – P) and ratios (P/O). Individual-level data (2017–2023; N=24,165 cases) were also analyzed for lineage and demographic shifts. A total of 4,295,302 influenza diagnoses occurred during the study period. During the policy, observed cases dropped by 248,781 cases in 2020 (P/O: 2.40; 95% prediction interval, 2.28 to 2.52), 204,619 in 2021 (1.72; 1.63 to 1.81), and 199,773 in 2022 (1.66; 1.58 to 1.76). Following policy relaxation in 2023, influenza cases exceeded expectations by 148,662 (0.8; 0.76 to 0.84); however, cases in children under 5 still declined by 22,388 (1.08; 1.04 to 1.12). B/Victoria lineage dominated early in the pandemic (>99% of cases in 2021), while influenza A/H3 (55%) and A/H1 (41%) emerged as the most prevalent strains by 2023. China’s Zero-COVID policy was associated with changes in influenza transmission dynamics, with explosive outbreaks after relaxation except among young children. Changes in lineage predominance among lineages intensified during and after restrictions, with uncertain long-term implications.
