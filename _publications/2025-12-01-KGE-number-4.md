Existing kernel methods for semiparametric models fail to properly account for spatiotemporal dependence structures inherent in the data, resulting in less efficient estimation. The complexity of these structures further poses methodological challenges for both inference and computation. To address these issues, this work proposes a novel profile-based approach for estimating these models. This approach  learns complex dependence structures without assuming stationarity by leveraging machine learning techniques. Unlike existing methods, it properly accounts for spatiotemporal dependencies in estimating both the nonparametric and parametric components of the mean function. Under a spatiotemporal increasing-domain asymptotic framework, we show that the proposed estimator reduces bias and improves efficiency relative to existing kernel estimators. It also ensures consistency and achieves optimal efficiency for the parametric component. To the best of our knowledge, this study provides the first large-sample evidence supporting the application of spatiotemporal semiparametric models integrated with machine learning for irregular spatial data. Simulation studies further demonstrate the proposed method's finite-sample performance and robustness, even when the covariance or data-generating process is misspecified. An analysis of real-world air pollution data further validates the effectiveness and efficiency of our methodology.
