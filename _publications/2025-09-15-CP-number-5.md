In real-world applications such as economic policy evaluation or biomedical monitoring, relationships between variables often experience structural breaks, posing major challenges for widely used partially linear models (PLMs). Existing methods for detecting such breaks in PLMs face significant methodological challenges, largely due to the complexity of nonparametric nuisance functions, unknown error distributions, weak signals, and potential correlations among predictors. This study proposes a novel tail-adaptive CUSUM (TAC) method for detecting structural changes in PLMs to address these issues. TAC introduces four key innovations to improve the robustness of change-point detection: (1) it integrates cross-fitted composite quantile regression with a CUSUM process based on weighted composite loss functions; (2) it allows heterogeneous error distributions with flexible tail behavior, remaining effective under sparse signals and nonparametric interference; (3) we establish the uniform convergence of the proposed test statistic to its oracle counterpart, derive of its asymptotic properties, and achieve the optimal localization rate for estimating structural change-points; and (4) it enables theoretical scalability to high-dimensional settings through a well-designed penalized estimation procedure. Extensive simulations and real-world data applications demonstrate the effectiveness and robustness of the proposed approach.
